# ADR-0062 — Dimensional type: compiled typefaces and the first Overlay-owned body

## Status

**Canon (built and approved 2026-09-03: `headline-hands-on` passed Scott's gate on gfx-review — "looks pretty great" — was machined as one piece after it, and landed on `main` at `417fad0`; the 3D Canvas Upgrade epic `sh9b6qxd` closed on `dev` the same day).** The second body of [ADR-0059](0059-compiled-stage-models-and-the-physical-screen.md) and the first one an Overlay owns, arriving in the Workspace of [ADR-0060](0060-the-stage-in-the-workspace.md) as a row, a canvas entity, and an inspector on its first day. Amends ADR-0059 §2 (materials) and answers the open questions of the `stage-bodies` Brief.

Date: 2026-09-03

## Context

Phase 2 of the 3D Canvas Upgrade named two bodies: a real model and real 3D type. The model shipped as the physical screen ([ADR-0059](0059-compiled-stage-models-and-the-physical-screen.md)) and its lane — compiled assets, a body pass with a material model, a shadow map, ambient obscurance, the focus-pull rule, ceilings — was built general. What the type needed was the geometry of a headline: the Pack's own face turned into glyph volumes, placed where an Overlay would sit, lit and shadowed with everything else.

Scott asked whether [pmndrs/glyph](https://github.com/pmndrs/glyph) made sense. It does not: it is a two-dimensional text stack — atlases, signed-distance fields, Slug-style analytic coverage — built Three-first, and adopting it would have put a second text engine beside the DOM text the Surfaces already set, for a problem it does not solve. A headline on the stage is not text rendered well; it is a body. The path decided on 2026-09-03: parse the Pack's face offline, take the contours, triangulate, extrude, and bevel in our own geometry lane, kern from the face's own pair tables, and ride the body machinery that already exists.

Two things the first frames exposed shaped the rest. Fonts draw over themselves — a stem and its foot, a serif and its stroke, an arch and its stem are one contour that crosses itself and relies on the nonzero rule — so a triangulator handed the raw outline dropped strokes and grew wedges. And the bevel, an inset ring, can cross a thin stroke at an acute corner. Both had to be resolved deterministically, with no boolean library in the browser.

## Decision

1. **Typefaces are compiled, never parsed at runtime.** `scripts/compile-stage-typeface.ts` reads a face the Pack already ships (a `@fontsource` WOFF2 cut) through `fontkit` and writes `src/lib/assets/typefaces/<slug>.stageglyphs` (`stage-glyph-format.ts`, GFXT v1): the headline glyph set's outlines, advances, and pair kerning from the face's own layout, with the face's units-per-em, cap height, x-height, ascender, and descender. `stage-typefaces.ts` registers each compiled face with its source file and sha256; `stage-typeface-assets.ts` loads and decodes one on demand; `StageTypefaceController` keeps the decoded faces resident and gates first paint and export, as `StageModelController` does for models. No font library reaches the browser. Five faces ship, one per Pack.

2. **Outlines are resolved by the compiler.** `stage-glyph-outline.ts` flattens curves at a tolerance in cap heights (the same at every size), splits every contour at its own crossings into simple loops (`splitRingAtSelfIntersections`), and the compiler unions the loops wound with the glyph's outer winding and subtracts the counters (`polygon-clipping`, a development dependency). The file carries simple rings as line commands. `stage-glyph-outline.test.ts` proves every glyph of every registered face triangulates its caps exactly; a face that fails cannot be registered.

3. **The geometry is ours and deterministic.** `stage-type-geometry.ts` shapes a line (advances plus pair kerning, in cap heights; a code point the face lacks is left out, never boxed), sorts a glyph's rings into outers and the counters they contain, triangulates the caps with `earcut`, and extrudes: the back cap on the plane, straight sides, a chamfer bevel over an inset ring, the front cap toward the eye — four material regions (face, bevel, side, back) in one `StageMeshData` the body pass draws like a compiled model. The mesh is in cap heights, centred on the line's visible extents, its baseline at y = 0. A headline is machined as one piece: every glyph on the line carries the same chamfer, the largest every contour can carry — a bevel never eats more than a share of a stroke's half-width, and an inset that crosses its stroke (an edge turning back, an area going the wrong way, a cap that no longer triangulates exactly) is retried smaller until it holds — while a hairline that can carry none extrudes straight on its own without costing the line its bevel. Counters move with their outer ring, so an O's inner chamfer is the same cut as its outer one. The same text in the same face at the same form yields the same bytes.

4. **Dimensional type is an Overlay whose body the stage draws.** `dimensional-type` (`src/lib/pipelines/overlays/dimensional-type/`) declares `stageBody: true` in its definition and contributes a body through the new `OverlayStageBodyRenderer` contract: from its content (text, size as a cap height's share of the frame's short side, depth and bevel in cap heights, lift and lean), the Pack, the decoded typeface, and the Overlay's progress, it returns the mesh, the materials, the placement terms, its presence, and that it pulls focus. `partitionStageOverlays` now returns `bodies` beside `shared` and `posed`; a body Overlay leaves every capture root (`Composition.svelte` excludes it from the shared plane and mounts no posed root), so no plane ever paints it — the stage owns its pixels. Off the stage it renders a flat DOM fallback in the Pack's face and ink, so a composition that drops the stage still reads. Its Editor exposes the same six fields the schema carries.

5. **A framed body stands where the mount would have put it.** `resolveOverlayPlacementCenter` (`src/lib/utils/overlay-placement.ts`) is the Overlay mount's placement law made analytic — an edge anchor insets the body's footprint by the placement offset, a centre anchor ignores it, a composition-owned motion channel shifts it — and `resolveStageFramedBodyModel` (`depth-stage-geometry.ts`) places the body by the posed-plane law of [ADR-0057](0057-filmed-canvas-camera-pose-and-posed-planes.md): the camera ray through that centre meets the page-parallel plane at the Overlay's signed depth, one cap height is `size` of the frame's short side there — the height of a wide frame, the width of a tall one, so the same headline reflows into the vertical frame — and the pose turns it about that centre. That law keeps a body at its frame size whatever the camera does, so a line that would run past the frame's safe width shrinks to fit it: a wide face or a tall frame never pushes a headline off the picture, and no Preset needs an orientation sibling to hold one. `lift` raises the body off its plane along the normal and `lean` pitches it back, both scaled by one minus the Overlay's progress: the settled-place entrance, landing on its plane as the enter completes and rising again on the exit. `stage-body-overlays.ts` is the one seam that resolves the composition's body Overlays into `DepthStageBody` draws — for the frame renderer and for the Workspace's canvas regions alike, so a body is selected exactly where it is drawn.

6. **Its materials come from the Pack, through Roles; ADR-0059 §2 is amended.** A model's plastics are intrinsic and Pack-immune; a headline is the Pack's voice, so its face and bevel take the Pack's ink and its extrusion the Pack's accent, as albedo under the Stage's material model (matte face and bevel, a rougher extrusion, no metal). Three Roles carry it: `dimensional-type.ink` (falling back through `field-ink-treatment` to `ink-treatment`, since a headline stands on the field), `dimensional-type.accent` (falling back to `accent-treatment`), and `dimensional-type.face` (a registered typeface slug; the reference face when a Pack omits it). Every shipped Pack claims all three. Object materials remain outside the Pack; a Pipeline-owned body's materials resolve through its Roles.

7. **It is a Workspace entity on day one.** The Overlay's row is the body's row; its canvas region is the projected silhouette of its resolved mesh through the renderer's own camera at the playhead (`projectStageMeshFrameBounds`), keyed `overlay-body:<id>`, selecting the Overlay and, at fit zoom, orbiting the camera like any body ([ADR-0060](0060-the-stage-in-the-workspace.md) §4). The inspector is the Overlay's inspector. `appearance.set-stage-*` and the layer Operations cover it without a new tool.

8. **It is the first consumer of the focus-pull rule.** The body declares `pullsFocus`; the lens racks to it by its presence as it lands and back as it leaves, exactly as ADR-0059 §8 reserved. Presence reaches one before the landing finishes, so the body is solid while it settles.

9. **What the first frames taught became engine capability.** Four things a thin, small, arriving body exposed that a large opaque monitor never did, each fixed in the engine rather than around the type. The shadow map's receiver offset and depth bias grow with the receiver's slope to the key, capped (`depth-stage-shadow.ts`): a grazing side had striped with its own stored depth. A body below full presence gets a depth pre-pass before its colour pass (`depth-stage.ts`): blended without one, its far surfaces showed through its near ones. The multisample resolve averages a pixel's depth samples where they lie on one surface and keeps the nearest across a contour (`depth-stage-body-pass.ts`), so a tilted face reads back as the plane it is. And the ambient obscurance subtracts a rise — a pixel and a half of slope plus two steps of the sidecar's 16-bit precision at that depth — before a tap counts (`depth-stage-ambient-occlusion.ts`): a face nearly square to the camera had read back as terraces a few pixels wide, and the spiral traced their walls as a hatch. None of it is type tuning.

10. **Ceilings and validation.** A composition may carry at most `STAGE_BODY_CEILINGS.maxBodies` body Overlays (a semantic validation error beyond it), and the stage's vertex, index, and byte ceilings sum over every present body. A headline's text is 1–64 characters.

## Consequences

- `headline-hands-on` is the Pack-neutral, orientation-neutral proving Preset: one headline on the field, lifted and leaning in, landing as the camera travels toward square. Its committed poster and render-matrix row are the acceptance evidence beside Scott's gate. The affected lane ran at the closeout on 2026-09-03 and could not measure the body: the matrix's readable and geometry authorities read the DOM, and on the stage this Overlay paints none, so its row reads unavailable (not failed). A body geometry and readable authority for the Stage is not planned (Scott, 2026-10-03); the poster and Scott's gate remain the acceptance evidence. The only failures in that lane are the known `crt-terminal` output-class quirk every opaque piece carries.
- The `stage-bodies` Brief's open questions are answered: Space Grotesk 700 is the reference face and every Pack names its own; depth and bevel are fractions of cap height authored per Overlay (0.35 and 0.06 by default); the body's shadow falls on whatever stands behind it along the key — the field, the page, or another body — through the shared shadow map, so the question dissolves.
- Only one typeface is decoded per composition: the Pack's. A composition that needs two faces on one stage is a Pack decision, not an Overlay field.
- A compiled face is not a rendering font: it carries the headline glyph set only (Latin, punctuation, figures), no shaping beyond pair kerning, no diacritic composition. A headline is not a paragraph.
- Open: dimensional type for the Block Layer (a figure standing in a chart) waits for a Brief; the Overlay contract is ready for it.
