## Context

By the end of 2026-09-02 the Stage renders a real object under a real camera: the FW900 CRT stands on its floor, the picture lights the room, the camera pushes in over the cut, and a vertical frame films it through its own pose. Scott approved the render and named the gap in the same breath: _"nothing is reflected in the UI. The timeline is just a surface."_ And the ambition behind it: _"I want this to work well enough that we can do some crazy stuff with it eventually."_

He is right about the Workspace. The timeline builds one row per Layer entity from composition state — Surface, Marks, Blocks, Overlays, captions, text animations, sound, video — and knows nothing of the stage: the camera travel and the rack focus exist only as fields in a stage section that is itself reachable only with nothing selected, inside the root inspector. The body is a mesh the canvas overlay cannot hit-test, because canvas selection picks DOM elements carrying selection attributes. The only three-dimensional gesture is grabbing the page to move the camera's aim at fit zoom; the wheel is unused; no gesture orbits or dollies. And a timeline drag — any timeline drag — records no undo entry today.

Dimensional type is the next body ([ADR-0059](0059-compiled-stage-models-and-the-physical-screen.md) consequences), and a third body after that. Each one added to a Workspace that cannot show the first compounds the gap. So the entity machinery comes first, built general: rows per body, selection by identity, gestures that write through the same decisions an agent makes.

## Decision

1. **Stage rows are timeline rows.** When the stage is on, the outline gains a **Camera** row and a **Focus** row at its top, and one row per **body** labelled with the model's own name. They are stage rows, a class beside the Layer rows and the Video row, minted from the same closed identity union (`stage-camera`, `stage-focus`, `stage-body` with a body id) so selection, focus, and parity all speak them. The Camera row's one clip is the **travel** window; the Focus row's one clip is the **rack focus**; a body's clip is its presence. Under a vertical frame the Camera row shows and writes the vertical travel when one is authored. No keyframed camera channels: [ADR-0057](0057-filmed-canvas-camera-pose-and-posed-planes.md)'s rest pose plus one travel holds, and the row is its face. Stage rows never compete with the Video row, which the schema already makes mutually exclusive with the stage.
2. **Stage entities are inspected by selection.** Selecting the Camera row opens the **Camera inspector** (move, pose, travel, vertical camera); the Focus row opens the **Focus inspector** (focus plane, aperture, band, rack focus); a body row opens that body's inspector — for the screen, its model. The root inspector's stage section keeps only the stage switch and the backdrop. Nothing is duplicated: the fields move, they are not copied.
3. **A body is an entity on the canvas.** Its resident mesh bounds, the placement that puts its glass on the Surface plane, and the same camera rig the renderer uses give a projected box; that box is a canvas selection region ranked below the page and the Overlays, so clicking the picture selects the page and clicking the housing selects the body. It draws an outline like an Overlay's, selects its row, and opens its inspector. This is an entity, not a scene tree: a body keeps the identity the Stage gives it, and no object hierarchy, node editor, or separate 3D workspace appears.
4. **The camera is orbited by hand.** At fit zoom, dragging a body orbits the camera about its aim (yaw from horizontal drag, pitch from vertical), the wheel dollies the distance, and grabbing the page keeps reframing the aim as it does today. Every gesture is clamped to `STAGE_CAMERA_POSE_LIMITS`, writes the pose the current frame films through (the vertical pose under a vertical frame), records exactly one undo entry, and uses the camera math in `depth-stage-camera.ts` — there is no second camera model, so what is grabbed is what exports. This amends [ADR-0057](0057-filmed-canvas-camera-pose-and-posed-planes.md)'s consequence that the aim is edited as a field only: the aim handle it removed re-projected the page under the pointer; orbiting the camera about a fixed aim does not.
5. **The camera and the focus are Operations.** `appearance.set-stage-camera` writes `/state/stage/camera` and `appearance.set-stage-focus` writes `/state/stage/focus`, beside `appearance.set-stage`, which still writes the stage whole; the `appearance` family already owns `/state/stage`, and the longest pointer wins. Each moves the Workspace focus to its row (`stage-camera`, `stage-focus`; a body write focuses `stage-body`). The gestures and the inspectors write the same fields these operations validate, so GUI and agent stay one surface ([ADR-0054](0054-webmcp-operation-transaction-and-security-contract.md)).
6. **Every timeline drag records one undo entry.** A clip or keyframe drag snapshots the open composition at pointer-down and at release, and records one history entry when they differ, invalidating autosave. The Camera row would otherwise have inherited a gap every other row already had.

## Considered options

- **A separate 3D panel or viewport for the stage** — rejected: the Workspace has one canvas and one timeline, and the stage is the space those already compose in ([ADR-0028](0028-dimensional-depth-stage.md)). A second workspace would fork selection, undo, and parity.
- **Camera keyframes as timeline channels** — rejected again, as in [ADR-0057](0057-filmed-canvas-camera-pose-and-posed-planes.md): a rest pose and one travel keep the vocabulary decisive and the inspector bounded (amended by ADR-0057 on 2026-10-14: a travel may chain up to eight whole moves, one clip each on the Camera row; per-field channels stay rejected). If a piece needs more, that is a new decision, not a row.
- **Keeping the stage in the root inspector and adding rows that only select it** — rejected: a row that opens a section for something else teaches the wrong model. Selection opens the entity.
- **Selecting the body only from its row, not the canvas** — rejected: the body is the thing on screen; a Workspace where the visible object cannot be clicked is the gap Scott named.
- **A generic `set-stage` write for the gestures** — rejected: the camera and the focus are the decisions a person makes on the canvas and an agent makes in a call; narrower rows give both a receipt that says what changed.

## Consequences

- `timeline-entity-identity.ts` gains three stage kinds; `composition-timeline-tracks.ts` gains `appendStageTracks` at the head of the order; the gutter labels, the inspector routing, `composition-workspace-focus.ts`, and the inventory's focus targets each learn them (the focus switch is exhaustiveness-checked, so a missed case fails the type check).
- `DepthStageSection.svelte` splits into `StageCameraInspector.svelte`, `StageFocusInspector.svelte`, and `StageBodyInspector.svelte` (keyed by body id, the screen today); the root keeps the stage switch and the backdrop; `stage-camera-editing.ts` holds the pose writers, the orbit and dolly conversions, and the rest pose the inspector and the gestures share.
- `CanvasEditingOverlay.svelte` gains a `stage-body` selection layer whose region is the body's projected silhouette (`projectStageBodyFrameBounds` in `depth-stage-geometry.ts`: every resident vertex through the renderer's own camera — a deep object's bounding box projects far wider than the object), fed by the resident mesh through `Workspace.svelte`; the orbit gesture and the wheel dolly; `canvas-interaction-geometry.ts` gains the layer rank.
- `composition-appearance-operations.ts` and `webmcp-appearance-tools.ts` gain the two operations, and the camera's tool schema carries the pose, the travel, and the vertical camera an agent could not set before; `pnpm audit:webmcp-parity` and `pnpm audit:parity` gate them.
- `TimelineOutline.svelte` and `VideoTimelineTrack.svelte` record undo for every drag through `captureCompositionGestureOrigin` / `recordCompositionGestureEdit` beside the transaction; the canvas gestures record through the same pair. A whole-document undo returns the selection to the composition root, as an operation's undo does.
- The human gate is the Workspace itself on gfx-review with `crt-filmed` open: rows, selection, orbit, undo.
- Dimensional type ([ADR-0059](0059-compiled-stage-models-and-the-physical-screen.md)) follows and arrives as a body row, a canvas entity, and an inspector on day one.
